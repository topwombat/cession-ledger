The Cession Ledger

How Native land passed to the United States, treaty by treaty, 1784 to 1893, and what the government's own Indian Claims Commission later found it was worth against what was paid. Prototype: prices for part of the country so far.

What the map shows

Each shape is one cession area as numbered by Charles Royce for the Bureau of American Ethnology (1899), drawn from the Forest Service's digital layer of Royce's maps. Indigo is land not yet ceded by treaty at the chosen year; red marks areas ceded in the five years before it. Areas are dated by their first cession; land ceded twice by different nations shows under its earliest date.

What the prices are

"Worth" is the Indian Claims Commission's finding, decades later and in litigation, of what the raw land was worth on the date it was taken. "Paid" is the Commission's finding of the consideration, with annuities converted as it did. They are adjudicated figures, not market prices, and each card names the decision it comes from.

What is missing

Prices exist so far only for the states named in the price view. Royce's Georgia, Tennessee, Iowa 2, Illinois 1 and three Indian Territory maps have no digital areas yet, so the Cherokee and Creek heartlands are not drawn. Cessions before 1784, and what the government later took in from each area, are not yet included. Acre tallies are computed from the drawn shapes.
